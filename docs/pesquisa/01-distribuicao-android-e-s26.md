# android-distribution: Android developer verification, Galaxy S26 dev setup, Play/iOS/Windows distribution, and foreground-service/notification rules for a workout timer (as of 2026-10-14)

## 1. What is the timeline for Google's Android developer verification, and is Brazil in the first wave?
[verified]
Yes, Brazil is in the first wave. The official timeline: March 2026, verification opened to all developers in Play Console and the new Android Developer Console (ADC). Around April to June 2026, an 'Android Developer Verifier' system service started rolling out to devices. July 2026, early access to limited-distribution accounts and the ADC API. August 2026, limited-distribution accounts, the ADC API and the power-user 'advanced flow' launched globally. September 30, 2026, enforcement began in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand on certified devices running Android 7+. Global expansion is planned for 2027, with no exact date. Google's June 18, 2026 update narrowed the first phase to installs from 'participating stores': Google Play, Samsung Galaxy Store, HONOR App Market, OPPO App Market (OPlus), Palm Store (Transsion), V-Appstore (vivo) and GetApps (Xiaomi). Google says this will 'soon expand to all third-party Android app stores'. Enforcement covers phones and tablets only. Play apps are mostly auto-registered: Google says over 99%, and Play App Signing users are claimed automatically. I found no announcement after Sept 30, 2026 that changes this timeline.
Sources: https://developer.android.com/developer-verification | https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides | https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/android-developer-verification.html | https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/android-developer-verification-rolling-out-to-all-developers.html | https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq

## 2. Does verification affect a developer installing his own app on his own phone via Android Studio Run, 'flutter run' or 'adb install'?
[verified]
No. ADB installs are exempt. The official FAQ says: 'As a developer, you are free to install apps without verification with ADB', 'there are no changes to how ADB works', and 'The waiting period does not apply to ADB installs.' The guides page says 'ADB workflow and experience stays the same.' Android Studio Run deploys through adb. 'flutter run' does too: the local Flutter tool source at C:\develop\flutter\packages\flutter_tools\lib\src\android\android_device.dart (around lines 441-446) installs with `adb install -t -r`. So debug, profile and release builds installed from the PC over USB or wireless adb need no verification, no Play account and no fee. One more detail: from Android Studio Panda 4, the IDE shows an app's registration status when you generate a signed APK or AAB.
Sources: https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq | https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides | https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/android-developer-verification.html

## 3. In Brazil today, what about installing an APK file directly on the phone (browser or My Files), without adb?
[likely]
Most likely still allowed in phase 1. The FAQ says: 'If you distribute your app through other stores, or if users sideload your app directly, these new verification requirements won't apply to your app yet.' It also says the Sept 30 deadline 'only applies to the specific participating stores.' Google's Mishaal Rahman (as reported) said the same. One caveat: the limited-distribution guide uses broader wording, saying unregistered package names will 'no longer be installable on certified Android devices' in those regions. There are two practical blockers, though. Samsung Auto Blocker blocks installs from unauthorized sources by default. And from the 2027 global rollout, an unregistered app will need adb, the advanced flow, or registration.

## 4. What is the free account for students and hobbyists, and what does a paid account cost?
[verified]
The Limited Distribution account in the Android Developer Console launched globally in August 2026. It is free, needs no government ID and no extra verification, and is meant for students, hobbyists and personal or non-commercial use. Requirements: a Google Account with 2-Step Verification, a linked Google payments profile (legal name and address) and a contact email. It lets you register package names and share apps with up to 20 authorized devices at a time. To add a device, you generate an authorization code. Then you scan the QR code with the device, or send a link that expires in 7 days. The device owner confirms they know and trust the developer. Unauthorized devices cannot install or update the app. Removing a device is permanent, so it must be re-authorized to come back. You can register only package names never seen before on Android. A limited account can be migrated to a full account, but not the other way round. A Full Distribution ADC account costs US$25. Signing keys matter: 'If you lose your signing key you won't be able to register your packages', and ADC supports multiple signing keys per package.
Sources: https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/limited-distribution | https://support.google.com/android-developer-console/answer/17131204?hl=en | https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq

## 5. What is the 'advanced flow' for power users installing unverified apps?
[verified]
It launched in August 2026 and is a one-time setup. The steps: enable Developer options, pass an anti-coercion check, then restart and re-authenticate. A one-time 24-hour wait follows. After that you confirm with biometrics or PIN and choose whether to allow unverified installs for 7 days or indefinitely. Each install still shows an 'unverified developer' warning with an 'Install Anyway' button. Developer options can be turned off afterwards. There is no adb command to skip the wait. Updates to unregistered apps work only while the advanced flow is enabled or through adb.
Sources: https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq | https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/android-developer-verification.html

## 7. How do you enable Developer options, USB debugging and wireless debugging on the S26?
[verified]
Developer options (Samsung path from Android docs): Settings > About phone > Software information > tap Build number 7 times. Developer options then appears at the bottom of Settings; keep its master toggle on. USB debugging: Settings > Developer options > USB debugging. Then accept the RSA prompt on the phone when connected. On Windows, install the Samsung OEM USB driver; Flutter's Android setup page says this. Wireless debugging needs Android 11+ and the PC on the same Wi-Fi network. Go to Developer options > Wireless debugging, and choosing 'always allow on this network' makes it a trusted network. Pair once, either with Android Studio's 'Pair devices over Wi-Fi' QR dialog or with `adb pair ip:port` plus the 6-digit code. Pairing persists until you Forget the PC or revoke authorizations. Troubleshooting: `adb server-status` should report version 37.0.0+ and mdns_enabled true. On Android 17 with adb 37.0.0 there is 'adb Wi-Fi 2.0', which reconnects automatically on a trusted network. A Wireless debugging Quick Settings tile is available under Developer options > Quick settings developer tiles. Once adb sees the device, 'flutter devices' lists it.
Sources: https://developer.android.com/studio/debug/dev-options | https://developer.android.com/tools/adb | https://docs.flutter.dev/platform-integration/android/setup

## 8. What is Samsung Auto Blocker, and how do you disable it for development?
[likely]
Auto Blocker lives in Settings > Security and privacy > Auto Blocker. It blocks apps from unauthorized sources and 'commands by USB cable', plus malicious images in messaging apps and unofficial software updates. Samsung says it is on by default on devices with One UI 6.1.1. The S26 shipped later, on One UI 8.5, so it is very probably on by default, but I did not find an official S26 statement. Users and vendor pages consistently report that USB debugging is greyed out or switched off while Auto Blocker is on, and turning it off fixes this; Samsung's own page does not mention adb. On One UI 8.5, turning it off reportedly offers a timed pause that re-enables it after 30 minutes, with an option to keep it off. This comes from teardowns and community posts, not Samsung docs, so check for a 'Turn on automatically' sub-option. On One UI 9, the opt-in 'Maximum restrictions' mode reportedly cuts off the USB connection entirely (beta reports). Keep Maximum restrictions off while developing. I could not confirm whether Auto Blocker also blocks wireless adb.

## 9. What other Samsung gotchas apply (battery management killing timers or foreground services, notifications)?
[verified]
Samsung's developer page says apps unused for about 3 days that affect system health become 'sleeping'. Sleeping means a restricted bucket that limits Jobs, Alarms and foreground services. After about 16 days of non-use (subject to change) an app becomes 'deep sleeping': it runs only when opened, with no background work or notifications. Users can exempt an app under Settings > Battery (Device care) > Background usage limits > 'Never sleeping apps', and should also consider turning off 'Put unused apps to sleep'. Apps can deep-link to these lists with action com.samsung.android.sm.ACTION_OPEN_CHECKABLE_LISTACTIVITY, package com.samsung.android.lool, and extra activity_type (0 = sleeping, 1 = deep sleeping, 2 = never sleeping). Samsung states: 'Since One UI 6.0, foreground services of apps targeting Android 14 will be guaranteed to work as intended', provided they follow Android's foreground-service type policy. dontkillmyapp.com still lists Samsung as aggressive, but its data is older (2024). Notifications follow standard Android 13+ rules: POST_NOTIFICATIONS is off by default for new installs.
Sources: https://developer.samsung.com/mobile/app-management.html | https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10003442/ | https://dontkillmyapp.com/samsung

## 10. What does a new personal Google Play developer account need (fee, closed testing, identity)?
[verified]
A one-time US$25 fee paid by credit or debit card; prepaid cards are not accepted. You must be 18+. Identity verification takes a government ID in your legal name if your Google payments profile isn't already verified. Contact email and phone are verified by one-time code. New personal accounts must also verify they have a real Android device by using the Play Console mobile app. Personal accounts created after Nov 13, 2023 must run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in for at least 14 consecutive days before they can 'Apply for production'. A tester who opts out resets the count. The application is a questionnaire about the test, the app and production readiness. Some third-party sites claim Google checks tester engagement more strictly in 2026; Google's page does not say so. The internal testing track allows up to 100 testers and delivers builds within minutes, and may skip standard reviews. Google does not explicitly say the 12/14 rule applies to internal testing. Target API: from Aug 31, 2026, new apps and updates must target Android 16 (API 36), and you can request an extension to Nov 1, 2026. Organization accounts need a D-U-N-S number and are exempt from the 12/14 rule.

## 11. Can an iOS build be produced from Windows? What about CI options, Apple fees and free provisioning limits?

## 12. How do you distribute a Flutter app on Windows (MSIX / Microsoft Store), and what does the account cost?
[verified]
Building for Windows needs Visual Studio (not VS Code) with the 'Desktop development with C++' workload (ID Microsoft.VisualStudio.Workload.NativeDesktop), which is missing on this machine. The pub.dev 'msix' package (v3.18.0) runs as `dart run msix:create`. It signs with a self-signed test certificate by default, can produce a Store build with --store (the Store then signs it), or an App Installer package for distribution outside the Store using your own certificate. Microsoft Store individual registration has been free since Sept 2025 in nearly 200 markets: the US$19 fee is waived in the new flow. You must start at storedeveloper.microsoft.com, since other entry points show the legacy paid flow, and verify with a government ID plus a selfie. A May 2026 Windows blog post says the US$99 company-account fee was also removed.

## 13. Which foreground service type fits a workout rest timer or an ongoing workout notification on Android 14-17?
[verified]
From Android 14, every foreground service must declare a type. Each type needs FOREGROUND_SERVICE plus its own permission, and apps targeting 14+ must declare the types in Play Console > App content. 'health' is defined as 'long-running use cases to support apps in the fitness category such as exercise trackers', so it fits a whole workout session. It needs FOREGROUND_SERVICE_HEALTH plus at least one of: HIGH_SAMPLING_RATE_SENSORS declared in the manifest, or a granted runtime permission from BODY_SENSORS (API 35 and below), READ_HEART_RATE, READ_SKIN_TEMPERATURE, READ_OXYGEN_SATURATION or ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION. 'shortService' needs no extra permission and runs about 3 minutes from startForeground(). Calling startForeground again extends it by 3 minutes, but only while the app is visible or exempt. You must implement onTimeout() and stop, or risk an ANR. It is not sticky and does not exist on Android 13 and lower. 'specialUse' needs a <property> justification that Play reviewers read. 'dataSync' is the wrong type: on Android 15 its services share a combined 6-hour budget per 24 hours and it cannot start from BOOT_COMPLETED. The Flutter package flutter_foreground_task (v11.0.3; Flutter 3.44+ / Dart 3.12+) supports health and shortService. Android 17 adds a rule for apps targeting API 37: audio played or audio focus requested from the background fails silently unless the app has a foreground service with while-in-use capabilities, or holds the exact alarm permission and uses USAGE_ALARM streams.
Sources: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types | https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/timeout | https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-15 | https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-17 | https://pub.dev/packages/flutter_foreground_task

## 14. What are the POST_NOTIFICATIONS and exact-alarm rules, and are there notification alternatives (Live Updates)?
[verified]
POST_NOTIFICATIONS (Android 13+): notifications are off by default for new installs. Apps targeting 13+ choose when to show the prompt; ask in context. If the user denies it, foreground-service notifications don't appear in the notification drawer, only in Task Manager. Media-session notifications are exempt. Exact alarms: SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM is not pre-granted on new installs of apps targeting 13+; the user grants it under 'Alarms & reminders'. Check canScheduleExactAlarms() and send the user to ACTION_REQUEST_SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM. If the permission is revoked, the app's future exact alarms are cancelled. USE_EXACT_ALARM is auto-granted but restricted by Google Play to apps whose core function is precise timing (alarm, timer or calendar apps), and Play reviews it. setExact with an OnAlarmListener needs no permission but only works while the process is alive. Inexact alarms can be delayed: on Android 12+, setWindow windows are at least 10 minutes. In flutter_local_notifications (v22.3.2; Flutter 3.38.1+; desugaring and compileSdk 35+ required) the AndroidScheduleMode values are exact, exactAllowWhileIdle, inexact, inexactAllowWhileIdle and alarmClock. AndroidNotificationDetails supports usesChronometer, chronometerCountDown, when, ongoing, timeoutAfter and audioAttributesUsage, but has no Live Update or promoted-ongoing property. Android 16 Live Updates require: Standard, BigText, Call, Progress or Metric style; the non-runtime POST_PROMOTED_NOTIFICATIONS permission; setRequestPromotedOngoing; ongoing; a content title; and no custom RemoteViews. 'Starting a workout' is listed as an appropriate use. The status-bar chip can show a chronometer countdown. Samsung's Now Bar has shown Android 16 Live Updates from third-party apps since One UI 8 (press reports), and OEMs may add their own criteria.
Sources: https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission | https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms/schedule | https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms | https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241 | https://pub.dev/packages/flutter_local_notifications | https://pub.dev/documentation/flutter_local_notifications/latest/flutter_local_notifications/AndroidScheduleMode.html | https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/live-update | https://www.androidauthority.com/one-ui-8-live-updates-support-3573794

## IMPLICATIONS
- Answer to 'Android Studio or multiplatform?': distribution does not force native. Phase 1 needs no store at all. The dev loop is 'flutter run' or Android Studio Run over USB or wireless adb, which Google explicitly exempts from developer verification, even in Brazil after Sept 30, 2026. It costs nothing and needs no Google Play account.
- Fix the machine before coding. In Android Studio go to SDK Manager > SDK Tools and install 'Android SDK Command-line Tools', then run `flutter doctor --android-licenses`. Install the Samsung USB driver on Windows. On the S26, enable Developer options (tap Build number 7 times), USB debugging and Wireless debugging. Pair once with Android Studio's QR dialog. Turn off Auto Blocker under Security and privacy (and its auto turn-on option if present), and never enable Maximum restrictions while developing.
- Choose a unique, permanent applicationId on day 1 (not com.example.*), such as a reverse domain the user controls. Create a release keystore and back it up off the PC. Registering a package name for verification is tied to its signing key, and Google says a lost key means you cannot register the package.
- Future-proofing for the 2027 global rollout: register the package name in a free Limited Distribution account in the Android Developer Console. This lets the app be installed and updated without adb on his phone and up to 19 other devices (friends, or a Fatec evaluator if that is real). Requirements: a Google Account with 2-Step Verification and a payments profile; no ID and no fee. This is cheaper and simpler than Play production.
- Defer Google Play production. If wanted later, budget US$25, ID plus device verification, a closed test with 12+ testers for 14 consecutive days, target API 36 (required since Aug 31, 2026), foreground-service type declarations and a Data safety form. The internal testing track (up to 100 testers) is a quick way to get Play-delivered updates on his own phone.
- Set compileSdk/targetSdk 36 now and test on Android 17, which his S26 most likely runs after the Sept 30, 2026 Brazil rollout of One UI 9. Plan for targetSdk 37: rest-end sounds should come from a notification-channel sound, not app-played audio from the background, unless a while-in-use foreground service is running.
- Recommended rest-timer design, more robust than Gemini's 'stopwatch' plan. (1) Persist the timer as an absolute end timestamp in the DB, so it survives process death. (2) Show the countdown in the app UI. (3) When the app goes to background, post an ongoing notification with usesChronometer and chronometerCountDown, with `when` set to the end time. (4) Run the whole 'workout in progress' session as a foreground service of type health, with ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION requested in context, or HIGH_SAMPLING_RATE_SENSORS declared. For rest periods only, shortService (about 3 minutes) is an alternative. (5) For the 'rest over' alert, use an exact alarm when canScheduleExactAlarms() is true. Otherwise fall back to the foreground service's own in-process timer. USE_EXACT_ALARM is acceptable for a sideloaded personal build but is a Play-policy risk if he publishes. (6) Request POST_NOTIFICATIONS in context, when the first workout starts.
- Samsung-specific UX: on first workout, show a one-time tip with a deep link (com.samsung.android.sm.ACTION_OPEN_CHECKABLE_LISTACTIVITY, package com.samsung.android.lool, activity_type = 2) to add the app to 'Never sleeping apps'. Otherwise Samsung's sleeping-app buckets can delay reminders, such as weekly body-measurement reminders, once the app goes unused for about 3 days.
- Optional polish: an Android 16+ Live Update (promoted ongoing notification with a chronometer chip) for an active workout would appear in Samsung's Now Bar. flutter_local_notifications currently lacks promoted-ongoing support, so this needs a small Kotlin platform channel. Treat it as a later enhancement.
- iOS: it cannot be built on this Windows PC. Keep the code multiplatform-ready with Flutter, but postpone iOS. When needed, use Codemagic (500 free macOS minutes per month) or GitHub Actions macOS runners (free for public repos). Installing on a real iPhone without a Mac effectively needs the US$99/year Apple Developer Program plus TestFlight. A free Apple ID gives only 7-day installs on up to 3 apps and 3 devices, and requires Xcode on a Mac. Brazil's iOS 26.5 alternative marketplaces do not help a personal app.
- Windows/PC: install Visual Studio with the 'Desktop development with C++' workload to unlock Flutter Windows builds. Package with the 'msix' package (v3.18.0). Microsoft Store individual registration is free via storedeveloper.microsoft.com with ID plus selfie, but the Store is unnecessary for personal use.
- Treat Gemini's 'Radar Health' GPS and 'Smart import via Gemini API' as unrelated to distribution. Nothing in 2026 distribution policy blocks them, but they add Play declarations (location, Data safety) if he ever publishes.
