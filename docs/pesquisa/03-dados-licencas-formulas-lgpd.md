## 9. US Navy body-fat formula (metric): inputs, women, error
[verified]
Hodgdon & Beckett 1984 (Naval Health Research Center). All measurements in cm. MEN: %BF = 495 / (1.0324 − 0.19077·log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456·log10(height)) − 450, with waist (abdomen) taken at the navel. WOMEN: %BF = 495 / (1.29579 − 0.35004·log10(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100·log10(height)) − 450, with waist at the natural (narrowest) waist and hip at the widest point of the gluteals. Inch-based DoD 1308.3 equivalents: men 86.010·log10(abdomen − neck) − 70.041·log10(height) + 36.76; women 163.205·log10(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684·log10(height) − 78.387. BODY WEIGHT IS NOT AN INPUT, so the Gemini description ('height, weight, waist, neck') is wrong; weight is used only afterwards to derive fat mass and lean mass in kg. Women also need hip. The IOM 1998 book renders the women's term as '(abdomen I + hip + neck)', which looks like a typesetting artifact; the DoD coefficients and every implementation use '− neck'. Error: IOM 1998 reports R = 0.90 and SEE = 3.52 %BF for men, R = 0.85 and SEE = 3.72 for women; the original men's cross-validation gave r = 0.90 and SE 2.70 %BF. So expect roughly ±3.5–4 points for an individual. Guard against waist ≤ neck (log of a value ≤ 0). Since 2022 DoDI 1308.03 lets each military service pick its method, and the Army's newer one-site test (abdomen + weight) has coefficients I could not verify from an official source.
Sources: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/6104/chapter/4 | https://med.libretexts.org/Courses/Irvine_Valley_College/Physiology_Labs_at_Home/03%3A_Anthropometrics/3.02%3A_Part_B-_Circumference_Measures/3.2.04%3A_Part_B4-_The_U.S._Navy_body_fat_estimation_formula | https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA143890.pdf | https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA370158.pdf | https://www.airandspaceforces.com/dod-services-more-options-measuring-body-composition/

## 10. Epley and Brzycki 1RM estimates and valid rep ranges
[likely]
Epley (1985, 'Poundage Chart', Boyd Epley Workout): 1RM = w·(1 + r/30), for r > 1 (r = 1 returns w). Brzycki (1993 JOPERD; also his 1998 book): 1RM = w·36/(37 − r). It is undefined at r = 37 and grows steeply at high reps. The two are identical at r = 10; below 10 reps Epley gives slightly higher estimates, and they diverge strongly above about 10. Validation (LeSuer et al. 1997 JSCR; Mayhew et al. 2008 via secondary summaries): equations are more accurate when based on sets of fewer than 10 reps to failure, and accuracy varies by lift (deadlift was underestimated). Estimates can be off by 10% or more, especially for novices. A 2026 arXiv preprint (Marzagao) proposes a weight-dependent equation fitted on 303,494 app-logged sets; it is not peer-reviewed. Practical rule: compute e1RM only for sets of 1–10 reps (some sources say ≤ 12).
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-repetition_maximum | https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17495

## 11. Mifflin-St Jeor BMR and activity multipliers
[verified]
Mifflin MD, St Jeor ST, et al., 'A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals', Am J Clin Nutr 1990;51(2):241-7, doi:10.1093/ajcn/51.2.241. Units: kg, cm, years. Men: 10·W + 6.25·H − 5·A + 5. Women: 10·W + 6.25·H − 5·A − 161. Frankenfield et al. 2005 (J Am Diet Assoc 105(5):775-789) found it the most accurate of the common equations, within 10% of measured RMR for the largest share of people, but individual errors remain and some age and ethnic groups are underrepresented. Usual activity multipliers: 1.2 sedentary, 1.375 light, 1.55 moderate, 1.725 active, 1.9 very active. These are convention and do not come from the Mifflin paper; treat TDEE as a starting estimate and adjust it from weekly weight trends.

## 12. RIR vs RPE
[verified]
The resistance-training RPE scale is anchored to reps in reserve (Zourdos et al. 2016, JSCR 30(1):267-275, doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000001049; Helms et al. 2016, Strength Cond J): RPE 10 = 0 RIR, 9 = 1 RIR, 8 = 2 RIR, 7 = 3 RIR, so RIR ≈ 10 − RPE. Half points such as 9.5 mean 'maybe one more rep'. Accuracy: the Halperin et al. 2022 Sports Medicine meta-analysis (12 studies, 414 participants) found people under-predict reps to failure by about 0.95 reps on average, with very high heterogeneity. Predictions are slightly better closer to failure, with heavier loads, in later sets and in sets under 12 reps.
Sources: https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/efef3b25-6701-4fb5-bb82-55fcd2a26027/full | https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/67 | https://www.strongerbyscience.com/reps-in-reserve

## 13. Double progression
[likely]
Pick a rep range (for example 3×8–12) at a fixed load. Add reps session to session until ALL sets reach the top of the range, then raise the load by the smallest practical increment and restart at the bottom. A related heuristic is the '2-for-2 rule' (NSCA/Baechle, attribution only seen in secondary sources): raise the load when the last set exceeds the target by 2 or more reps in 2 consecutive sessions. No trial tests 'double progression' under that name. Plotkin et al. 2022 (PeerJ, 43 trained subjects, 8 weeks) found progressing load and progressing reps gave similar hypertrophy and strength (squat 1RM about +20 kg in both groups).
Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9528903/ | https://www.mycg.uscg.mil/News/Article/2910464/total-force-wellness-column-guidelines-to-progress-your-physical-training-over/ | https://alphaprogression.com/en/glossary/double-progression

## 14. Weekly sets per muscle: evidence summary
[likely]
Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017 (J Sports Sci 35:1073-82, doi:10.1080/02640414.2016.1210197; 15 studies, 34 groups): each extra weekly set was linked to about +0.37% muscle growth (P = 0.002). The categorical result (<5, 5–9, ≥10 sets per week) was only a trend (P = 0.074). Pelland, Remmert, Robinson, Hinson & Zourdos 2026 (Sports Med 56(2):481-505, doi:10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w; 67 studies, 2,058 participants): both hypertrophy and strength rise with weekly volume, with diminishing returns that are much steeper for strength. Frequency clearly matters for strength but not for hypertrophy. Counting indirect sets as 0.5 ('fractional' sets) predicted outcomes best. A related lab paper reports no detectable benefit beyond about 31 fractional sets per week for hypertrophy; I have not verified that figure in the final article.
Sources: https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Dose-response%20relationship%20between%20weekly%20resistance%20training%20volume%20and%20increases%20in%20muscle%20mass%20-%20A%20systematic%20review%20and%20metaanalysis.pdf | https://sponet.de/sponet/Record/4097139?lng=en | https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460

## 15. Android: where to store private progress/body photos; do image_picker or camera write to the gallery?
[verified]
App-specific internal storage (Context.filesDir): no permissions needed, other apps cannot access it, encrypted on Android 10+, deleted on uninstall. The MediaStore scans only shared folders (DCIM/, Pictures/, Movies/, Download/, ...), so photos in app-specific dirs do not appear in Samsung Gallery or Google Photos. Android docs say media that only has value inside the app belongs in app-specific storage. Flutter mapping (path_provider_android source): getApplicationSupportDirectory() → filesDir, getApplicationDocumentsDirectory() → getDir('flutter') (app_flutter), getTemporaryDirectory() → cacheDir. image_picker 1.2.4: camera captures are saved to the app's local cache and are 'expected to only be around temporarily'; the plugin uses scoped storage with no manifest setup, and on Android 13+ picking uses the system Photo Picker (no READ_MEDIA_IMAGES needed). camera 0.12.1 (CameraX implementation): takePicture() writes File.createTempFile(...) into getCacheDir(), not the gallery. Neither plugin calls MediaStore; writing to the gallery takes an explicit call such as the gal package (2.3.3). Not verified: whether some OEM camera apps launched by image_picker's ImageSource.camera also keep their own gallery copy; the in-app camera plugin avoids that risk. Backup: Android Auto Backup includes filesDir, getDir(), databases, shared prefs and getExternalFilesDir by default and excludes cache and noBackupFilesDir. The quota is 25 MB per app; above it the cloud backup is skipped. Backups are end-to-end encrypted on Android 9+ with the screen lock. Use dataExtractionRules (Android 12+) plus fullBackupContent (11 and lower) to choose cloud vs device-transfer content. For export, write a ZIP of the DB, photos and a manifest to a user-chosen location (file_picker 13.1.0 saveFile on Android/iOS) or hand it to the share sheet. FLAG_SECURE to hide photo screens from screenshots and recents is standard Android, though I did not re-verify it here.
Sources: https://developer.android.com/training/data-storage/app-specific | https://developer.android.com/training/data-storage/shared/media | https://developer.android.com/identity/data/autobackup | https://pub.dev/packages/image_picker | https://pub.dev/packages/camera | https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flutter/packages/main/packages/camera/camera_android_camerax/android/src/main/java/io/flutter/plugins/camerax/ImageCaptureProxyApi.java | https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flutter/packages/main/packages/path_provider/path_provider_android/lib/src/path_provider_android_real.dart | https://pub.dev/packages/gal | https://pub.dev/packages/file_picker

## 16. LGPD (Lei 13.709/2018) implications if the app syncs to a cloud or is published
[verified]
Art. 4 I: LGPD does not apply to processing 'realizado por pessoa natural para fins exclusivamente particulares e não econômicos', so a local-only app for his own data is outside it. Once other people use the app and the developer or his backend handles their data, he becomes the controller. Art. 5 II lists 'dado referente à saúde' as sensitive data (dado pessoal sensível); weight, body-fat %, measurements, diet and body photos should be treated as such. Art. 11 I: the practical legal basis for a fitness app is consent given 'de forma específica e destacada, para finalidades específicas'. In practice that means a separate opt-in screen, not buried in the terms, revocable at any time, and no sharing for advertising. Also required: a privacy policy (Google Play requires it in-app and in the console, plus the Data safety form and the Health apps declaration that every app must file; fitness apps fall under 'Activity and Fitness'). Data-subject rights under Art. 18 include access, correction, deletion and portability, which an export feature and an account-deletion option cover. Art. 46 requires security measures. Art. 48 requires notifying incidents to ANPD and the users; ANPD Resolução CD/ANPD 15/2024 sets 3 business days. Cloud servers outside Brazil (for example Firebase) count as international transfer (Arts. 33-36, Resolução 19/2024, standard contractual clauses). Small-agent relief (Resolução 2/2022: no mandatory encarregado/DPO, doubled deadlines) is lost for high-risk processing, defined as one general criterion (large scale, or significant impact on rights) plus one specific criterion, and sensitive data is a listed specific criterion. Sanctions under Art. 52 go up to 2% of Brazilian revenue, capped at R$50 million per infraction. Sending PDFs or photos to the Gemini API ('smart import') or collecting GPS ('Radar Health') adds third-party sharing and location data that would need their own disclosure and consent.
Sources: https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm | https://www.mattosfilho.com.br/unico/regulamento-incidente-seguranca/ | https://www.mattosfilho.com.br/?p=107482 | https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/publications/2022/02/regulation-for-the-application-of-the-general-data-protection-law-lgpd-for-small-business-data-processing-agents/legal-update--regulamento-agentes-de-pequeno-porte.pdf | https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14738291?hl=en | https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469

## IMPLICATIONS
- Exercise catalog: ship a small hand-written PT-BR seed list (roughly 60–120 common Brazilian gym names such as 'Supino reto', 'Puxada frontal', 'Leg press 45°', 'Cadeira extensora', 'Mesa flexora'). Give each entry an English alias and primary/secondary muscle tags; free-exercise-db text is public domain and can be reused freely. Do NOT bundle free-exercise-db images (scraped, with an upstream warning against commercial use) or ExerciseDB GIFs (the terms forbid storing). This replaces Gemini's '20+ exercises with GIFs' seed.
- Make the user's own photo of each machine plus a structured 'machine settings' record (seat height, backrest angle, pin or hole number, pad position, handle, cable height, free notes) the visual reference instead of stock GIFs. It is the core value, it needs no license, and it fits the paper-notebook workflow. Model settings per exercise and per gym (machines differ between units).
- If stock images are wanted later, use wger content with license fields stored per item (license id, license_author, source URL) and an in-app 'Créditos/Licenças' screen. Only 383 images exist and only 66 PT translations, so treat it as optional enrichment, not the backbone.
- Nutrition: bundle TACO 4th ed. (597 foods, per 100 g) converted from the official NEPA xlsx at build time. Handle 'Tr' (trace), 'NA' and blank values as distinct states rather than 0, and show 'Fonte: TACO 4ª ed., NEPA/UNICAMP, 2011' in the app. Do not bundle TBCA (no reproduction, alteration or commercial use without permission).
- Barcode scanning: query Open Food Facts at runtime only on scan (stay under 15 product reads/min). Set a User-Agent 'AppName/Version (contact)' using a project contact address, credit OFF and the ODbL license, and fall back to quick manual entry because Brazilian coverage is about 37k products. Cache scanned items locally for personal use; if the merged food DB is ever published or shared, ODbL share-alike applies to it.
- Body evolution: implement the Navy formula correctly (inputs: sex, height, neck, waist; plus hip for women; no weight). Use weight only to derive fat and lean mass. Label results as an estimate of about ±3.5–4 points, validate that waist > neck, and store the raw circumferences so formulas can be recomputed later.
- Training analytics: estimate e1RM only from sets of 10 reps or fewer (show Epley and/or Brzycki and label them as estimates). Store RIR per set (RPE = 10 − RIR). Use double progression for 'next-session targets' (an improved Gemini 'Ghost mode': suggest +reps until every set hits the top of the range, then +load at the smallest increment the machine allows). Show weekly sets per muscle using fractional counting (primary = 1, secondary = 0.5) as an informational dashboard, not a prescription.
- TDEE/diet: Mifflin-St Jeor times a conventional activity factor as the starting point, then adjust calories from the 2–4-week weight trend. Show a 'not medical advice / consult a nutritionist' disclaimer (also needed for Play's health policies).
- Photos: save directly to getApplicationSupportDirectory()/photos (filesDir). Copy the image_picker or camera XFile out of the cache immediately and delete the temp file. Never call gal or MediaStore for body photos, and use the Android Photo Picker for imports. Optionally set FLAG_SECURE on photo screens.
- Backup: the 25 MB Auto Backup cap means photos will break cloud backup. Exclude the photos folder from cloud-backup with dataExtractionRules plus fullBackupContent, keep the SQLite DB included, and build an explicit 'Exportar backup' ZIP (DB + photos + JSON manifest, optional password) saved via SAF or the share sheet, with a matching import. Photos in app storage are deleted on uninstall, so the export is mandatory.
- LGPD strategy: stay offline-first and local-only for the personal phase (outside LGPD under Art. 4 I). Before publishing or adding cloud sync: separate explicit consent for health and photo data, a privacy policy, the Play Data safety form and Health apps declaration, in-app export and delete, a server region and contracts covering international transfer, and an incident process. Drop or make opt-in Gemini's 'Smart import via Gemini API' (sends health documents to a third party) and 'Radar Health' GPS (adds location data, no core value). Replace the mock admin login with no login at all for the local version.
- Treat the Fatec course context as unconfirmed; it does not change any of the licensing or LGPD conclusions above.
